EQ-1 · Operator → Timesheet Propagation

Equipment time is a first-class entity (EQ-0) decoupled from labour timesheets. When the operator is also a labour worker on our payroll, that decoupling leaves a hole: their hours can land on equipment time, but never reach payroll, billing, or cost-rollup correctly. This doc lays out the options for closing that hole and the trade-offs of each — for business review, not for implementation.

Jira: Q21030-11722 Driver: Mkwa (Sam Kelly, Marie Michano, Elaine Stewart) Related: EQ-0, UIS-15H, UIS-15H-0, Q21030-11803, Q21030-11808 Status: Pre-decision

The scenario

Day in the life — what Ian's loom shows

An operator opens the mobile app. They log time on the 235 link excavator for two hours under a job. They're operating it themselves. Today:

  • The mobile equipment-time form has no operator picker (Q21030-11803 is fixing that). Desktop already has one.
  • Once 11803 lands, they can put themselves down as the operator on the equipment time entry.
  • But: nothing then carries those two hours into their TimeSheetDetailRecord. Payroll reads TSR/TSDR/TSDRV — equipment time is invisible to it. The operator's pay quietly underpays unless they also remember to log a separate labour timesheet entry covering the same period.

The flip side — if they do log both an equipment time entry (as operator) and a TDR for those same two hours — there is no link between the records. Downstream consumers can now see both, and depending on the consumer, that's either a no-op or a double-bill / double-count.

What's already true (load-bearing givens)

1. EQ-0 — Equipment time is intentionally decoupled from labour timesheets

From the EQ-0 plan: "Equipment time is decoupled from the labour timesheet system — entries do not require a user or a TimeSheetDetailRecord. This handles all real-world scenarios: operator-logged time, unattended equipment (generators, pumps), dry hire, overhead/shop use, and foreman-logged entries for crew."

This decision is not on the table. Equipment time will keep its own model, own approval, own grid. We are designing on top of EQ-0, not around it.

2. operatorIncluded already exists and means wet hire

Schema: operatorIncluded Boolean @default(true) // wet hire vs dry hire. UIS-15H-0 makes the value inheritable from EquipmentAssignment, with a permission-gated per-entry override.

Wet hire = the equipment rate includes operator labour. Dry hire = equipment only, labour billed separately. This flag drives which EquipmentRateValue resolves, and so it already adjusts billing per entry.

Naming nuance — is internal-use really "wet hire"? The schema's wet/dry framing maps cleanly to the third-party scenario Elaine described ("we farm them out to Cress"). When the equipment is the company's and the operator is the company's and the work is on the company's own WO, calling it wet hire is a stretch — it's just an internal employee operating company equipment. The Top Option below pushes this observation further: even for the Cress case, itemized billing is the modern industry default, so operatorIncluded as a rate-affecting field may be unnecessary complexity inherited from a different problem domain.

3. JobBill already generates invoices from approved equipment time entries

Where: apps/api/src/models/JobBill.ts · generateEquipmentInvoice() at line ~1134.
Each EquipmentTimeEntry carries a jobBillLineItemId. Once invoiced, the entry is locked to that bill. operatorIncluded is passed to resolveEquipmentRate so the wet-hire rate is what bills.

4. WorkOrder→Invoice conversion already double-counts

Where: apps/api/src/models/WorkOrderConversion.ts · resolveWorkOrderLineItems().
This is the path used when a Work Order is turned directly into an invoice. It pulls both TimeSheetDetailRecordValue rows (as labour line items) AND EquipmentTimeEntry rows (as equipment line items) and merges them into a single line-item list.
Concrete double-bill risk today: if an operator logs 8h on an excavator (operator-included, wet hire) and 8h on their timesheet, resolveWorkOrderLineItems currently emits an equipment line for the wet-hire excavator rate plus a labour line for the operator's trade-level billable rate — for the same 8 hours. Client gets billed twice for the same labour.

5. Financial Summary already includes equipment costs in the main rollup

Where: apps/frontend/src/components/FinancialSummary/ — main rollup grid + EquipmentBreakdown.tsx drill-in panel.
Equipment costs feed the main Financial Summary via the EquipmentCategory → AccountItem chain. We seed a default Equipment Time AccountCategory with an Equipment Hours AccountItem, so every instance gets equipment hours rolled up into the main financials grid alongside labour, materials, etc. The dedicated Equipment Breakdown panel is an additional drill-in — not the only surface where equipment cost shows up.
So the cost-rollup risk is real today, not just future: if propagation is turned on without dedupe, an operator's hours land in Equipment GL (via the seeded AccountItem) and Labour GL (via the new shadow TDR) at the same time. Both the main rollup AND the Equipment Breakdown drill-in need the dedupe rule. This is tightly linked to Q21030-11808's cost-code rollup work.

The three lenses any solution must satisfy

Any propagation design has to produce a coherent answer for these three consumers. The hard part is doing it consistently across all three at the same time, especially when operatorIncluded changes the right answer for billing and cost-rollup.

Truth table

Consumer What it needs operatorIncluded = true (wet hire) operatorIncluded = false (dry hire)
Payroll
(reads TSR/TSDR/TSDRV only)		 The operator's hours must reach a TDR so their wage is paid for those hours. Operator paid their normal wage for those hours. Operator paid their normal wage for those hours.
Billing
(WO→Invoice + JobBill)		 Each hour billed exactly once at the correct rate. Bill the wet equipment rate only. Do NOT also bill the labour line — it's already inside the wet rate. Bill the dry equipment rate plus a separate labour line at the operator's billable trade-level rate.
Cost rollup
(Financial Summary, 11808)		 Each hour costed exactly once in the right GL bucket. Cost sits in Equipment GL (the wet rate's cost component contains the operator wage internally). Do NOT also book the operator's hourly cost to Labour GL. Cost split: dry rate → Equipment GL, operator's hourly cost → Labour GL.

Key observation: the payroll column is the same in both cases — operator must be paid. The billing and cost-rollup columns diverge based on operatorIncluded. Two ways to handle this divergence: (a) branch every consumer on the flag (the B-alt approach below), or (b) remove the divergence at the data layer so consumers never see it (the Top Option below). The Top Option is meaningfully simpler.

What clients have actually asked for

Cress has the equipment, but they needed an operator for the day. Our guys, we look at the timesheets — like if they come in and they're working that day as a tri-axle truck driver, I need that one because then that tells me they're [paid] at that rate."

Reading the room

  • Mkwa's mental model is timesheet-centric for invoicing. They want to see equipment time alongside labour time in a daily view because that's the artifact they bill from.
  • Operators do mixed-mode days — 15 min inspection, 2 hr excavator, 4 hr admin, 1.75 hr paperwork. Mobile UX must handle that gracefully.
  • Worker rate varies by activity — same worker may bill as a tri-axle truck driver on Tuesday and a labourer on Wednesday. Already handled via TradeLevel rates on TDR, but the question is which rate applies when the activity is operating equipment.
  • Wet-hire to a 3rd party is a real flow for them (their guys go out with Cress's equipment, or vice versa). The operator's wages don't follow the equipment ownership — they're a Mkwa employee either way.

What Atlas does not say

Mkwa has not explicitly described what they want when the equipment is theirs and the operator is theirs and the rate is wet hire (the double-billing scenario). They've described it indirectly — "we invoice off timesheets" implies they don't expect both lines on the invoice — but no one has explicitly said "bill the wet rate, suppress the labour line." We should validate this in the next Mkwa call before locking the design.

★ Top Option — Drop wet/dry rate divergence; equipment time as a labour breakdown

Eliminate operatorIncluded as a rate-affecting field at the data model layer. Equipment rates are always equipment-only (effectively always "dry" pricing). When an operator runs equipment, their hours live on a TimeSheetDetailRecord like any other labour. The EquipmentTimeEntry for those hours back-links to the TDR via a new timeSheetDetailRecordId field and represents the equipment cost only. Mobile gets a unified flow: timesheet form gains an "Equipment used" breakdown section under each labour line; standalone equipment-time form is kept (unchanged) for foreman-logging, unattended equipment, and maintenance-only meter capture.

Why this changes the conversation: the double-bill / double-count problem doesn't need dedupe rules because the data model never represents an hour in two billing buckets. Equipment time = Equipment GL + Equipment invoice line. TDR = Labour GL + Labour invoice line. Always. No back-link logic, no wet/dry branching on the consumer side.

What gets created when an operator logs their day

  1. Operator opens the mobile timesheet form (the primary entry point — matches Marie's mental model).
  2. They log time entries as today: job + cost code + trade level + hours.
  3. For any entry where they were operating equipment, they tap + Add equipment and attach one or more EquipmentTimeEntry rows — each with the equipment, how many of that row's hours were on that machine, and (optionally) meter readings.
  4. Submit produces: one TDR + TDRVs for the labour hours, and one EquipmentTimeEntry per attached equipment with timeSheetDetailRecordId set.

What gets created when a foreman logs equipment for a crew, or equipment runs unattended

  1. They open the standalone Equipment Time form (mobile or desktop — same UX as today, plus Q21030-11803's operator picker on mobile).
  2. If both an operator AND a job are selected, submit auto-creates a paired TimeSheetDetailRecord + TimeSheetDetailRecordValue for that operator on that job + date, and links the equipment entry to it via timeSheetDetailRecordId. The labour hours default to the equipment hours (operator was on the machine for that duration); the operator can edit their TDR later if their actual labour day was longer or shorter.
  3. If operator is null (unattended / maintenance) or job is null (can't tie labour without a job), submit produces just the EquipmentTimeEntry with timeSheetDetailRecordId = null. Pure equipment / utilization / maintenance capture.

This covers Marie's common case: a foreman logs that Bob ran the excavator for 4h on Job X via the standalone form → Bob's timesheet automatically gets a 4h TDR for Job X. Bob doesn't have to touch his own timesheet for it to land in payroll.

Invariants the system enforces

  • Sum of equipment-hours across EquipmentTimeEntries linked to a given TDR cannot exceed that TDR's hours. "I worked 4h, was on the excavator for 3h" is valid; "I worked 4h, was on the excavator for 5h" rejects on submit.
  • A TDR-linked EquipmentTimeEntry inherits job + workDate from the TDR. It cannot be moved to a different job or date without also moving the TDR.
  • Editing / rejecting / deleting a TDR cascades to its linked EquipmentTimeEntries.
  • Editing the equipment portion happens via the TDR's equipment-breakdown UI, not the standalone equipment-time grid (the grid shows them read-only with a "from timesheet" badge).

Pros

  • Double-bill / double-count vanish at the data layer. No dedupe rules in WorkOrderConversion, no dedupe rules in Financial Summary, no special-casing in payroll exports.
  • Matches Mkwa's "invoice off timesheets" model exactly. The timesheet is the primary artifact; equipment is a breakdown attached to it.
  • Mixed-mode days collapse to one form. Marie's 15min inspection + 2h excavator + 4h admin example is a single timesheet, not a tab-switching exercise.
  • UIS-15H-0 simplifies dramatically. The operatorIncluded override permission + the inheritance logic become dead — they exist to swing the rate, and the rate is no longer swinging. isFullDayBilling stays (it's about per-period prorating, independent of operator).
  • The eq_hours ≤ labour_hours invariant is trivially enforceable and prevents whole classes of data-integrity bugs.
  • Standalone equipment-time path keeps working as EQ-0 designed. Unattended equipment, foreman-logged crew equipment, dry-hire-to-3rd-party all flow through it unchanged.
  • Industry-aligned with combined-domain T&M apps. Foundation, Sage Service Ops, ServiceTitan, etc. all itemize labour and equipment as separate dimensions. Bundled wet rates are an equipment-rental-only convention (United Rentals, Sunbelt).

Cons / tradeoffs

  • Bundled wet-hire pricing for 3rd-party farm-out (Elaine's Cress example) becomes itemized. Cress would see two lines on the invoice: equipment rate + operator rate, instead of one bundled wet rate. Two mitigations: (a) the invoice template layer can roll up adjacent equipment+labour lines into a presentation-only bundled line, with the underlying data still itemized; (b) if Mkwa really wants bundled at the data layer, they can configure a higher equipment rate that includes the operator portion and just not log the operator on those entries — same outcome.
  • Migration for any currently-configured wet-hire rates. Existing wet rate cards become "equipment rate" cards; labour rates that match the operator's TradeLevel need to be configured separately so the sum matches. Today effectively only Mkwa is on this feature, so the migration is small if we move now and brutal if we wait until 10 clients are configured.
  • Two semantic flavours of EquipmentTimeEntry (TDR-linked vs standalone) require the equipment-time grid to distinguish them visually + behaviourally. Manageable, but worth being explicit about in EQ-2.
  • Trade-level rate question remains — when an operator is operating equipment, do they bill at the same trade level as their other labour, or a separate one? This is a configuration question independent of this option's structure. (See Q2 below.)

Alternatives considered

Four other approaches were evaluated against the same three lenses. Each is kept here for contrast so the trade-offs of the Top Option are visible.

A · Status quo (do nothing)
Lift: 0

Equipment time and labour stay decoupled. The operator is expected to log both a TDR and an equipment time entry manually for hours they spend operating their own machine.

Cons:
  • Operator under-pay when they forget to log labour separately (today's actual bug — Ian's loom).
  • Today's WorkOrderConversion already double-bills if they remember both.
  • No mobile UX to handle mixed-mode days.

Verdict: Not viable. This is what Ian is reporting as broken.

B-alt · Auto-create a linked, tagged shadow TDR (keep wet/dry rate divergence)
Lift: Medium

Was the recommended approach before the Top Option above emerged. Kept here for contrast — its added complexity is exactly what the Top Option avoids.

When an EquipmentTimeEntry is submitted with an operator selected (and an includeInTimesheet flag — see UX below), the backend creates a paired TimeSheetDetailRecord + TimeSheetDetailRecordValue for the operator on the same job, same date, at their default trade-level rate. The TDR carries a back-link: sourceEquipmentTimeEntryId.

That back-link is the load-bearing piece. It lets every downstream consumer make the right call:

  • Payroll reads TDR as today. Shadow TDR shows up like any other labour entry → operator is paid.
  • WorkOrderConversion → invoice: when sourceEquipmentTimeEntryId is set AND the source entry's operatorIncluded = true, skip emitting the labour line (it's already in the wet equipment rate). When operatorIncluded = false, emit it (dry hire = bill labour separately).
  • Financial Summary / 11808 GL rollup: apply the same rule — skip Labour GL booking for shadow TDRs whose source is wet hire.
  • Edits / approvals: the shadow TDR's hours always mirror the source entry. If the source is edited, rejected, or deleted, the shadow follows. The operator cannot manually edit a shadow TDR — they must edit the source equipment entry.
Pros:
  • Payroll keeps reading TDR as the single source — no surgery to Foundation, Sage50, Quickbook, DayforcePowerpay, Avantage, etc.
  • Matches Mkwa's "invoice off timesheets" mental model — equipment-sourced hours appear in the timesheet view alongside regular labour, visually flagged as equipment-sourced.
  • Wet/dry hire branching lives in one place per consumer (the back-link + the source's operatorIncluded flag), not scattered across UI.
Cons:
  • Two records of the same hours — discipline required to keep them in lockstep (edit cascades).
  • Every billing/cost-rollup consumer must learn to check the back-link + operatorIncluded. Initial audit pass is non-trivial.
  • Trade-level rate question: shadow TDR needs a rate. Default to the operator's default trade level, but does the operator's rate change when they're operating equipment? Open question for Mkwa.
C · Unified mobile "Day Entry" primitive
Lift: High

Largely subsumed by the Top Option above — the Top Option's "timesheet form with equipment-used breakdown" is essentially Option C with the standalone equipment-time form preserved alongside it (rather than consolidated away). Kept here only to make the contrast explicit.

The original C approach was a fully consolidated form replacing both pages/timesheets.tsx and pages/equipment-time.tsx with a single polymorphic flow. The Top Option backs off from full consolidation because the standalone equipment-time form is still needed for foreman-logging, unattended equipment, and maintenance capture — collapsing it into the timesheet form would force every standalone use through a fake-user TDR.

D · Read-side projection (no physical TDR)
Lift: High

Equipment time entries with an operator are projected as virtual TDR rows at query time (via a DB view or a backend resolver layer) without creating a physical TDR. Timesheet views UNION the real TDR rows with these virtual ones. Payroll exports get the same union.

Pros:
  • Single source of truth — never a sync problem.
Cons:
  • Every payroll export (Foundation, Sage 50, QuickBooks, Dayforce, Avantage, Paydirt, Spectrum, Computer Ease, etc.) would have to learn the union pattern. We have 8+ integrations.
  • Virtual rows can't be approved/rejected/edited via the timesheet UI — confusing for accounting clerks.
  • Trade-level rate still has to be resolved somehow, but now lazily.

Verdict: Theoretically cleanest, practically expensive. Option B achieves the same end-state outcomes with less integration surgery.

Open questions for business review

The following decisions can't be made by engineering. They need Travis/Corey, Mkwa, and (where called out) Sales/Implementation input before the Top Option can be locked in. Questions that were specific to the B-alt (wet/dry rate divergence) approach have been dropped — they become moot once the data model stops branching on operatorIncluded.

Q1 — Equipment-used breakdown affordance on mobile: always-visible, permission-gated, or instance setting?

Option: always render the "+ Add equipment" affordance under every labour line · Or: only when the operator has the EquipmentTimeEntry___Create permission · Or: instance-level toggle ("This company tracks equipment via timesheets").

Mkwa wants it always-visible. Companies that don't use equipment time at all shouldn't see the affordance. Permission-gating is probably the cleanest answer since EQ-0's mobile permission seeding already exists.

Q2 — When the operator is operating equipment, what trade-level rate applies to their TDR?

Their default trade level? A separate "equipment operator" trade level the admin configures? Whichever they're already using on other TDRs for that day?

Elaine specifically mentioned different rates for "tri-axle truck driver" vs other roles. The mechanism already exists — TimeSheetDetailRecord.overrideTradeLevelId lets a TDR override the user's default trade level on a per-record basis. It's wired up on the desktop timesheet form but is not yet surfaced on the mobile timesheet form. Surfacing it as part of EQ-2 is the cleanest path: the operator can pick which TradeLevel applies to each section directly on the form, with their default selected initially. Same control answers the per-section question for non-equipment lines too (the tri-axle driver vs labourer case).

Q3 — Cress farm-out invoicing: bundled "wet" rate on the invoice, or itemized?

When Mkwa rents their excavator + operator OUT to Cress, does Cress's invoice show one bundled line (e.g. "$185/hr Wet Rate × 8h = $1,480") or two itemized lines ($150 equipment + $35 labour)?

Under the Top Option, data is always itemized — bundled presentation would be an invoice-template concern (a "roll up adjacent equipment+labour lines into a presentation line" template option). 5-minute conversation with Elaine to confirm she's OK with itemized as the default, and whether she wants the rollup template as a follow-up.

Q4 — Edit cascade: if a TDR is rejected or deleted post-submission, what happens to its linked equipment time entries?

Cascade-delete the linked equipment time? Detach it (becomes standalone)? Block the TDR deletion until the equipment time is resolved?

Cleanest answer: cascade follows TDR. Editing the TDR's hours can adjust equipment hours pro-rata or trigger a review prompt. Once payroll has run on the TDR, neither side should be deletable without an explicit reversal flow — same constraint as today's TDR/payroll story.

Q5 — Equipment time for maintenance-only / unattended equipment: standalone form only, confirming?

Should not appear in the timesheet flow at all. timeSheetDetailRecordId stays null. EQ-0 already supports this path explicitly.

Confirming by design — this is the standalone form's continued purpose. No change to how it works today.

Q6 — Migration of existing wet-hire rate configurations

Any customer with configured wet-hire rates needs those split into (a) equipment-only rate + (b) corresponding labour rate on the operator's TradeLevel that together sum to the old wet rate. We'd run a one-time migration + flag for review.

Today effectively only Mkwa is on this feature and they're still in beta — migration is contained to one customer's rate cards and is more of a flag than a blocker. Worth doing the work now while the surface area is small.

Q7 — Implementation sequencing

The Top Option's pieces have to ship together to avoid leaving the system in an inconsistent state:

  1. Schema: add EquipmentTimeEntry.timeSheetDetailRecordId; deprecate operatorIncluded as a rate-affecting field (keep as informational column or drop entirely).
  2. Rate resolution: collapse wet/dry → single equipment-only rate path.
  3. One-time data migration: re-fit existing wet rate cards into equipment + labour rate pairs.
  4. Mobile timesheet form: add the "Equipment used" breakdown sub-section.
  5. Mobile + desktop standalone equipment-time form: keep as-is (plus 11803's operator picker on mobile).
  6. Desktop equipment-time grid: distinguish TDR-linked rows visually + behaviourally (read-only badge with deep-link to TDR).
  7. WorkOrderConversion.resolveWorkOrderLineItems and Financial Summary: no changes needed — that's the win.

Recommend bundling 1–6 into a single EQ-2 plan + PR. Pieces 1–3 are backend; 4–6 are UI. They can be reviewed in stages but should land together to avoid mixed configurations in production.

Recommended path forward

  1. This week: circulate this doc with Travis + Corey. Get directional approval on the Top Option's core premise — drop wet/dry rate divergence, equipment time becomes a labour breakdown when an operator is involved.
  2. Next Mkwa call: walk Sam / Marie / Elaine through the mockup and validate: (a) is itemized billing on Cress farm-out OK (Q3), (b) what trade-level rate applies to operator-on-equipment and is per-section overrideTradeLevelId on mobile the right UX (Q2).
  3. After answers land: convert the Top Option into an implementation plan — EQ-2 spec covering the seven pieces in Q7. Migrate Mkwa's existing wet-hire rate configs as part of the rollout.
  4. If the Top Option is blocked for a reason that surfaces during review (e.g. Mkwa hard-requires bundled wet-hire pricing on Cress invoices and the template-layer workaround isn't acceptable), fall back to B-alt — the tagged-shadow-TDR approach with dedupe rules. B-alt achieves the same lensed outcomes at higher complexity and is still strictly better than today.
