Discount Management

Buy-side supplier discounts — list price − discount = net cost — built by extending the existing Discount + DiscountAssignment system for procurement. Per-supplier, cascading per-cell › material › category › global, feeding bestAvailableCost().

Parent: Release 1.33 Slice: MAT-9 Est: 3–4 days
This is not a new model — it enriches what’s already shipped. Appello already has a mature, polymorphic discount system: Discount (the value object — Percent/FixedAmount, value, effective dates, ad-hoc) + DiscountAssignment (polymorphic referenceModel/referenceId, stacking, per-assignment override), both on BaseAuditableModel, with helpers forEntity/syncForEntity/attachOrRevive/createAdHocAndAttach/promoteAdHoc. Today it is 100% sell-side (customer Company, Job, Estimate/CO, JobBill, line items). MAT-9 adds a buy-side direction and two new scope targets so the same system models negotiated supplier discounts. The CEO draft’s standalone DistributorDiscount model (and its manufacturerProductId, which referenced the never-built MAT-0 entity) is dropped — it duplicated Discount field-for-field.

1 · Business Case & Customer Evidence ▼

Contractors buy off supplier list prices (price books, which rarely change) and negotiate a discount sheet per supplier that yields their actual net cost. Discount sheets are organised by product category, occasionally per-SKU. Without structured discounts, PMs hand-calculate net prices on every order — error-prone and margin-blind. The ask is consistent and simple: a place to record “X% off this supplier’s list, by category”, effective-dated, that flows into cost everywhere.

Customer Evidence (Atlas)

“We work off of just standard price lists from all the… suppliers… and they just provide us discounts. All you need is a place to input that discount percentage.” — Scott Norton, Thomas Group (onboarding, May 2026)
“They send you list price. Then there’s typically a discount sheet, which gives you your actual net price. Every company we work with has a different discount sheet.” — Corey Shelson, Appello (Thomas onboarding)
“You might order from the same supplier, but because of your volumes, might be able to offer different discounts from your rep.” — Appello Connect Office Hours (Jun 2026)

(The 2023 “Price Sheet” entity no longer exists — pricing was refactored into MaterialGroup → Material → MaterialAttributePrice. “Price-sheet level” maps onto MaterialGroup (category) today.)

Industry standard

This is the textbook ERP purchase trade agreement / line-discount-group model (Dynamics 365 et al.): discounts keyed by (vendor × item-group) with a specificity cascade Table › Group › All — exactly MAT-9’s per-cell/material › category › global. Crucially, the standard keeps sales and purchase agreements as separate concerns — which is why MAT-9 introduces an explicit direction rather than overloading the sell-side assignments.

Value Proposition

🎯
One discount, any scope
Build a reusable named discount (“Crossroads 2026 Global”) once; attach it global, per-category, per-material, or per-cell. The tier is just what you attach it to.
⚡
Cascading net cost
Most specific wins automatically — cell › material › category › global — resolved into bestAvailableCost(), so every estimate/PO/P&L reflects the real negotiated cost.
📅
Effective-dated
Reuses Discount.effectiveFrom/To — expired discounts are retained for audit but excluded from resolution.
🔒
Buy/sell never collide
A direction flag means a Company that’s both customer and supplier can carry both kinds of discount with zero ambiguity.

2 · Scope ▼

In Scope

  • IN Discount gains direction (Sell|Buy, default Sell) + nullable distributorId → Company (the supplier, set when Buy)
  • IN DiscountAssignment gains two new referenceModel values: Material and MaterialGroup (added to discountReferenceModels + delegateByReferenceModel)
  • IN Per-supplier discount library — reusable named buy-side discounts (isAdHoc:false), pickable wherever needed; ad-hoc inline (“type a one-off %”) remains via the existing createAdHocAndAttach/promoteAdHoc
  • IN resolveBuyDiscount(distributorId, materialId, mapRow) cascade resolver — per-cell discountId › Material › MaterialGroup › Company(global); most specific wins; direction:Buy + active-date filtered
  • IN Wire the resolver into MaterialAttributePrice.effectivePrice + MaterialPricingService.bestAvailableCost() (the MAT-0 seam) and the resolvedSellPrice SQL mirror
  • IN Supplier Company detail: Discounts tab (the library + scope assignments); discount picker at the source-price row (per-cell tier, the MAT-0 discountId FK)
  • IN Effective-price / expired display reuses Discount.effectiveFrom/To

Out of Scope

  • OUT Per-job discount override (Vanos “override within a Job, per sheet”) — deferred; see the purple note in §5. Natural future home: a buy-side DiscountAssignment(referenceModel:"Job").
  • OUT Volume / quantity-break discounts (buy X get Y%)
  • OUT Bulk price-book / discount-sheet import — MAT-3 (imports populate these same structures)
  • OUT Auto-application of discounts onto customer invoices; mobile discount management

3 · How it maps onto the existing system ▼

The two-dimensional problem — a discount is scoped by both which supplier and what it applies to — splits cleanly across the two existing models. The supplier lives on Discount; the scope target is the DiscountAssignment. The tier is which referenceModel you attach to:

Attach the Discount to…TierRankMechanism
the price row itselfper-cell4MaterialAttributePrice.discountId FK (shipped by MAT-0)
Materialper-material3DiscountAssignment{referenceModel:"Material"}
MaterialGroupcategory2DiscountAssignment{referenceModel:"MaterialGroup"}
Company (the distributor)global1DiscountAssignment{referenceModel:"Company"} (reused; disambiguated by direction:Buy)
Why this is the right reuse: a Discount is already a value object with everything a supplier discount needs (%, FixedAmount, name, effective dates, ad-hoc). DiscountAssignment is already 1:N, so one “Spring Promo 10%” can blanket several categories at once. The ad-hoc machinery already implements “type a one-off % inline, optionally promote to a named reusable discount later” — verbatim what customers asked for.
Per-cell uses an FK, not an assignment (decided): the per-cell tier is 1:1 with a price row and is read on every pricing resolution / the resolvedSellPrice SQL mirror, so MAT-0 carries it as a direct MaterialAttributePrice.discountId FK (cheap single-join reads, tractable SQL). The blanket tiers (1:N) use DiscountAssignment. The small asymmetry is deliberate.

DiscountAssignment — two new referenceModel values

No column changes. Add Material and MaterialGroup to the discountReferenceModels const-array (and the delegateByReferenceModel existence-check map). The full set becomes:

export const discountReferenceModels = [
  "Company", "Job", "EstimateChangeOrderRecord",
  "ItemizedEstimateChangeOrderRecordLineItem", "JobBill", "JobBillLineItem",
  "Material", "MaterialGroup"          // NEW (buy-side scope targets)
] as const;
The tier is the referenceModel. Buy-side assignments resolve in rank order Material(3) › MaterialGroup(2) › Company/global(1); the per-cell tier(4) is the MaterialAttributePrice.discountId FK from MAT-0. No scope column is needed — it’s implied by the target.

MaterialAttributePrice (from MAT-0)

The per-cell tier is the discountId String? FK → Discount already added by MAT-0 (which replaced the earlier flat discountPercent). MAT-0 resolves only that row’s own discount; MAT-9 generalises the resolution to the full cascade.

Deferred — per-job override. Vanos asked to “override the discount within a Job, per sheet.” This is a job-scoped buy-side override and is out of MAT-9 scope. It slots in later as a buy-side DiscountAssignment(referenceModel:"Job") consulted ahead of the supplier tiers when resolving cost for that job — no schema change beyond allowing Job on the buy side. Flagged so it isn’t lost.

6 · API Surface & Resolution ▼

GraphQL — extend, don’t add

The existing Discount / DiscountAssignment resolvers, queries, and mutations (createDiscount/editDiscount/deleteDiscount/promoteAdHocDiscount, plus the assignment sync) are reused. Additions:

enum DiscountDirection { Sell  Buy }

extend type Discount {
    direction: DiscountDirection
    distributorId: String
    Distributor: Company
}
# CreateDiscount / EditDiscount inputs gain: direction, distributorId

extend type Query {
    # the supplier's reusable library (isAdHoc:false), for the pickers
    buyDiscountsForDistributor(distributorId: String!): [Discount!]!
}

Assignment writes go through the existing syncForEntity / attachOrRevive / createAdHocAndAttach against the new referenceModels — no new mutation shapes. Sell-side pickers filter direction:Sell; buy-side filter direction:Buy.

Resolution — resolveBuyDiscount()

One new resolver, the buy-side analog of the cascade. Returns the most-specific active discount for a (distributor, material, cell); null if none. Lives in the model/service layer so MCP + GraphQL + the SQL mirror agree.

async function resolveBuyDiscount(
    distributorId: string,
    materialId: string,
    mapRow: { discountId: string | null; materialGroupId: string }
): Promise<Discount | null> {
    // 4. per-cell — the row's own FK (most specific)
    if (mapRow.discountId) {
        const d = await activeDiscount(mapRow.discountId);   // direction:Buy + dates
        if (d) return d;
    }
    // 3→1. material → category → global, via buy-side assignments for THIS distributor
    const assignments = await discountAssignment.forBuyTargets(distributorId, [
        { referenceModel: "Material",      referenceId: materialId },
        { referenceModel: "MaterialGroup", referenceId: mapRow.materialGroupId },
        { referenceModel: "Company",       referenceId: distributorId },   // global
    ]);   // joins Discount, filters direction:Buy + active dates
    // pick the highest-rank match: Material > MaterialGroup > Company
    return pickMostSpecific(assignments);
}
Where it plugs in: MaterialAttributePrice.effectivePrice and MaterialPricingService.bestAvailableCost() call resolveBuyDiscount() instead of reading the row’s lone discountId. The resolvedSellPrice TableFeed SQL mirror gets the equivalent cascade (a correlated lookup over DiscountAssignment joined to Discount, direction='Buy') so list views match the service — the MAT-0 plan already flags this hand-off.
Effective price = list − discount. Percent → base × (1 − value/100); FixedAmount → max(0, base − value), where base = listPrice ?? value. Same formula MAT-0 ships for the single-discount case — MAT-9 only widens which discount is chosen.

8 · Acceptance Criteria ▼

  • A buy-side Discount (direction:Buy + distributorId) can be created, named, effective-dated, and reused — it appears in that supplier’s library and nowhere in sell-side pickers.
  • The same discount can be attached at Global (Company), Category (MaterialGroup), Material, or per-cell (price-row discountId); one discount may have several assignments.
  • resolveBuyDiscount() returns the most-specific active match: per-cell › Material › MaterialGroup › global; ties broken by specificity, expired/inactive excluded by date.
  • Net cost = list − resolved discount (Percent or FixedAmount), surfaced on the source row and flowing into bestAvailableCost().
  • resolvedSellPrice TableFeed column reflects the same cascade as the service (no list-view drift).
  • Ad-hoc inline discount entry still works (createAdHocAndAttach) and can be promoted to a named library discount (promoteAdHoc).
  • A Company that is both customer and supplier carries sell-side DiscountAssignments and buy-side Discounts with no collision.
  • Existing sell-side discounts are unaffected (all backfilled to direction:Sell; estimate/bill discount behaviour unchanged).
  • Validation: a Buy discount requires distributorId; Percent value 0–100 (existing rule); name required (existing rule).
